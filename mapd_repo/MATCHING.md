# Conservative loaded-tile road ambiguity

Before retaining a current road or taking a predicted road, mapd checks every way in the currently loaded tile against the current GPS fix. A candidate needs a positive OSM way ID, at least two nodes, and a successful existing `OnWay(fix, 2)` test. That test uses the projected distance, reported horizontal accuracy (with its existing 5 m floor), way width, and one-way/direction rule. Repeated tile records with the same way ID count once. Rank, road class, and posted limit do not resolve a tie.

No candidates produce `noMatch`. A new match still requires a sole candidate that passes the existing narrow selection threshold. A zero posted limit on a matched way remains `matchedNoLimit`.

At a junction, a previously confirmed road may retain its limit if the car is moving at least 3 m/s, the current fix has a positive bearing accuracy of at most 10°, and its heading is within 15° of the confirmed road in the existing travel direction. The retained way must pass the narrow match. A connected piece of the same named road can take over if it is also aligned, passes the narrow match, and has the same directional speed limit and conditional policy. Every other candidate must either be a connected piece of that road with the same limit tags or cross the heading by at least 50°. Heading cannot acquire a new road from ambiguity.

Otherwise two or more distinct IDs produce a neutral invalid `unknown` output. A heading change, uncertain heading, parallel competitor, or stale fix removes the continuity exception. On failure the state clears its current way, upcoming evidence, curve output, accepted/override limit, and numeric map limit before serialization. A way retained from another tile cannot be published when the loaded tile has no plausible way.

The packed synthetic-tile tests cover parallel and crossing competitors, prior sticky and predicted ways, empty tile and new-fix recovery, duplicate IDs, a sole road admitted only by the broad envelope, genuinely unique direction-sensitive one-way roads, and no posted limit. A 64/256/1024-way benchmark checks enumeration cost; it does not establish worst-case device performance or justify a spatial index.
